# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

macos

## Users

The primary user works on a Mac for long stretches and wants focus state to remain visible without keeping a full application window open. They need to capture a short queue of concrete tasks, finish them one by one during a Pomodoro session, and understand the current timer state at a glance.

## Product Purpose

Floatdoro is a local-first menu-bar Pomodoro timer with a persistent optional floating pill. It combines a highly visible countdown with a lightweight task queue so the user can stay in flow, mark work complete, and move immediately to the next task.

Success means the current countdown and task are readable in under a second, starting a session takes one click, and the timer remains correct through sleep, relaunch, and switching applications.

## Positioning

The focus timer that stays visible and carries the next task with it.

## Brand Personality

Direct, tactile, and calm. The product should feel like a purposeful physical timer translated into macOS: visible when needed, quiet when not, and satisfying to operate.

## Anti-references

Do not reduce the active timer to a tiny generic menu-bar glyph. Avoid dashboard chrome, productivity gamification, nested cards, decorative statistics, and controls that compete visually with the countdown.

## Design Principles

- The time is the landmark: the countdown is always the strongest visual element.
- One action advances the work: completing a task immediately reveals the next unfinished task.
- Persistent, never intrusive: the menu-bar pill is always available and the larger floating pill is optional.
- Native behavior earns trust: keyboard access, system notifications, launch at login, sleep-safe timing, and familiar macOS interactions.
- Local by default: tasks and settings remain on the Mac with no account or server.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use high-contrast text, large tabular timer digits, descriptive accessibility labels, keyboard-accessible controls, and system-provided focus behavior. Never rely on color alone to distinguish focus and break states. Motion is limited to short state transitions and must respect Reduce Motion.
